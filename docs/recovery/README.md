## Priors

- Default ("realistic"): beta ~ Gamma(shape 4, scale 1.5) clipped to [2, 15], a realistic human range.
- "wide" (the original spec prior): beta ~ Gamma(shape 3, scale 1.5) clipped to [0.5, 15].
- Both: alpha1, alpha2, lam, w ~ Beta(2, 2); p ~ Normal(0.15, 0.2).

## Why `w` is reported, not gated

`w` is information-limited per person. At 200 trials the observed-information standard error of `w` is about 0.22,
the same size as the spread of true `w` (sd 0.22), and it shrinks roughly as 1/sqrt(n):
with the wide prior, r(w) was 0.57 at 200 trials, 0.86 at 1000 and 0.90 at 3000.
The diagnosis ruled out the other explanations:

- Not the optimizer: fitted NLL was at or below the NLL at the true theta in all 100 fits, and 60 starts gave the same fits as 20.
- Not a trade-off: fitted `w` is uncorrelated with fitted `beta1` (-0.02), `lam` (0.10) and `alpha1` (0.10).
- Partly signal: near-zero betas carry almost no information about `w`, which is why the default prior changed. Tying beta1 = beta2 did not help.

The study question is the group-level change in `w` under time pressure (`delta_w`), which pools information across people.
That is gated by TM-08's simulated recovery of `delta_w` (truth -0.8 and 0, 95% HDI covers the truth), not here.

## Other notes

- `lam` and `alpha1` are poorly identified (r about 0.5 to 0.6) and often sit on a bound; they are reported, not gated.
- 400 trials is a single 400-trial session, not two joint sessions.
